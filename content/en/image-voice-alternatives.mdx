## "I'm paying by the image, and my backlog is big"

Most of the paid AI apps on this page price by a monthly image allowance. That
works well for a store that adds a few products a month, and less well for one
that starts with thousands of undescribed images. A three-star review on
AltText.ai's listing, from a merchant who still recommends it, shows how that
plays out — the developer's reply explains the store had processed "5,700+ images
in your first two days, which blew past the 5,000 credits in your Titanium plan."
A five-star review puts it more calmly: "we have a very large catalogue so we will
need several months to work through it."

Alternatives, by what you would rather pay for:

- **The backlog, once.** {APP_NAME}'s paid plans open with a First Sweep that
  writes your existing backlog up front, in days; the monthly allowance after that
  only covers new images. ${PRICING.keeper.toFixed(2)} or ${PRICING.curator.toFixed(2)} a month.
- **Credits that never expire.** Alt Text Generator AI's credits "never expire"
  and "roll over." Read its billing notes first — [our comparison
  covers them](/image-voice/vs-alttextgenerator).
- **Sheer volume.** Pixc's Max plan is $100 a month for 25,000 AI-written images.
- **Nothing at all.** YT SEO writes from a template, free and unlimited.

## "Every image of a product gets the same sentence"

That is what a template does — product title, variant, type — and it is the
method AltKing and YT SEO describe on their listings. On packshots it is close
to accurate. On detail shots, on-model photography and banners it repeats the
title, because a template cannot see what is different about each photograph.

Alternatives that describe themselves as looking at the image:

- **AltText.ai** — its homepage says its AI "automatically analyzes each image on
  your site." 4.5 across 141 reviews, Built for Shopify.
- **SEO HERO** — "image context + your brand + SEO keywords." 4.9 across 171
  reviews, Built for Shopify.
- **{APP_NAME}** — reads each image individually and writes what is in the frame.
  No reviews yet.

If you are on AltKing specifically, [our comparison](/image-voice/vs-altking)
covers when its template is genuinely enough.

## "I don't know whether the alt text I already have is any good"

Most of the apps on this page write; fewer check the quality of what is already
there. Of the ones we looked at, three describe doing it, in different ways:

- **ALTerator** grades every alt text on a 0–100 SEO score. It is new, with one
  review. [Comparison →](/image-voice/vs-alterator)
- **Pixc** scans for "missing or poorly written" alt text. Also one review.
  [Comparison →](/image-voice/vs-pixc)
- **{APP_NAME}** sorts every existing description into missing, junk, weak or
  good, with a vision-checked sample you read yourself — a verdict on whether it
  describes the photograph rather than a score for search.

Or check by hand first: the [ten-minute self-audit in our
guide](/guides/shopify-alt-text) needs no app and gives you a number any vendor
has to beat.

## "I actually need an SEO suite, not an alt text app"

- **StoreSEO** — meta titles, schema, sitemaps, indexing and alt text in bulk. 5.0
  across 745 reviews. [Comparison →](/image-voice/vs-storeseo)

Both carry the Built for Shopify badge. You can also run a suite and a specialist
side by side; they do different jobs.

## "My real goal is accessibility compliance"

**Avada Accessibility** is an accessibility widget covering WCAG, ADA, EAA and
BFSG, with "AI-generated Alt Text for screen readers" among its features. 5.0
across 314 reviews. Its listing does not say whether that alt text is written
into your catalogue or supplied by the widget, which is worth asking. Note that
no tool, ours included, can promise compliance.

## Before you switch

Changing apps does not change the alt text already in your store. An app that
writes into Shopify's alt fields leaves what it wrote there after you switch. So the first useful step, before
choosing anything, is finding out how much of it describes its image. [{APP_NAME}'s
audit does that for free](/image-voice) and writes nothing to your store — and if
the answer is "most of it," you may not need to switch at all.
