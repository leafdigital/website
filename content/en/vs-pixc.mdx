Pixc is cheap, it scales further than any other app we compared, and it already
does something many alt text apps skip: it looks for the bad alt text
you have, not just the missing kind. If your catalogue is enormous and your
photography is simple, it may well be the better buy. Here is how to tell.

## What Pixc actually does

From its App Store listing, in its own words:

> The app scans your store to find missing or poorly written alt text, then
> fixes everything automatically in bulk. Choose AI mode to generate natural,
> descriptive alt text from your product data, or Template mode for full control
> with preset settings.

Two writing modes, and both start from the same place: **your product data** —
titles, variants, the text you already have. The AI mode turns that data into a
sentence; the Template mode arranges it into a pattern you set.

That is a real product and a sensible one. It is also a different starting point
from ours, and the difference is the whole page.

## Where Pixc is better

- **Scale.** Its Max plan is $100 a month for 25,000 images — the lowest price
  per AI-written image of any app we compared. If you are describing a catalogue
  that size every month, Pixc is built for it.
- **Entry price for the job.** Mini is $8 a month for 500 images. Our paid entry,
  Keeper, is ${PRICING.keeper.toFixed(2)}.
- **It audits too.** Pixc's scan looks for "missing or poorly written" alt text,
  not only empty fields. We would be misrepresenting the category if we implied
  we are the only app that checks what is already there.

## What is actually different

**Pixc writes from what you already know about the product. {APP_NAME} writes
from what is in the photograph.**

For a packshot on white, those are close to the same thing — the product data
_is_ the description. The gap opens on every image that exists because the title
was not enough: the detail of the collar, the on-model fit, the lifestyle shot,
the banner. Product data cannot describe those, because the information in them
never made it into the product data. That is why somebody photographed it.

{APP_NAME} reads each image individually and writes what is in the frame, in
roughly 125 characters. Before it writes anything, it grades what you already
have four ways — **missing, junk, weak, good** — with a vision-checked sample you
read yourself.

Two smaller differences, both from the listings:

- **Undo.** Every {APP_NAME} change has a 30-day undo on every plan, free
  included, with a before → after trail. Pixc's listing does not mention undo or
  rollback. That is not the same as saying it has none — check before you rely on
  it.
- **Languages.** Pixc's listing is in English and does not say which languages it
  writes in. {APP_NAME} writes in the store's own language: English, German,
  French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch and Portuguese.
