Nobody arrives on this page because AltText.ai writes bad alt text. By most
accounts it does not — 4.5 stars across 141 reviews. People usually arrive
because of the meter, or because a number they were shown did not match what
they could see.

## What its own reviewers say

The most useful review on AltText.ai's listing is a three-star one, edited on 30
June 2026, from a merchant with a large catalogue. It describes both of those
problems — and then how they settled:

> After the initial installation, we had some discrepancy issues between the
> "number of images without alt text" and the "number of images resolved." …
> This led us to be concerned about inflated credit usage. After giving it some
> time to process through all of our images, the daily reported issues seem to
> be much more accurate. Despite the initial headaches, I would still recommend
> this tool if you have a large cache of images you are trying to process.

The developer's reply underneath explains the credits: the store had processed
"5,700+ images in your first two days, which blew past the 5,000 credits in your
Titanium plan."

Read fairly, that is a good review of a tool that works, with two lessons in it.
A count of images "resolved" is only useful if it matches what actually changed.
And on a metered plan, a large catalogue spends its allowance fast.

A five-star review from 2 April 2026 makes the second point without complaining
at all:

> Works well - we have a very large catalogue so we will need several months to
> work through it.

That is a satisfied customer describing what a monthly allowance means for a
backlog.

## So what is actually different

**AltText.ai writes alt text. {APP_NAME} grades what you already have first,
then writes it.**

We are not the only app that checks existing alt text — ALTerator scores it out
of 100, and Pixc scans for "poorly written" descriptions. What {APP_NAME} does is
narrower: it reads what is already in your alt fields, whatever wrote it, and
sorts it four ways — **missing, junk, weak, good** — with a vision-checked sample
you read yourself. A typical result reads "62 of 100 covered images don't
describe the image." That is a sentence a count of filled fields cannot produce,
and it speaks directly to the first lesson in the review above.

Three more differences:

- **Nothing reaches your store unapproved.** Every description goes through a
  keyboard queue built for 200 decisions in one sitting — approve, edit, skip.
  Auto-pilot exists, and it is offered only once your own approval record
  justifies it.
- **Thirty-day undo, on every plan including free.** Full before → after trail.
- **The quality is gated, not trusted.** Writing is checked against a fixed
  golden set, so no update can quietly degrade what you approved, and a weekly
  re-scan keeps your number true.
